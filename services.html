What I have designed, built, and shipped.

These products required different kinds of thinking: organising a feature-rich Mac workspace, visualising connected infrastructure, and bringing reading, scanning, and audio together on iPhone. Their use of AI is different too—each one applies it where it genuinely improves the product.

CodeMenu · macOS

Organising a feature-rich Mac app without losing clarity.

I designed and built CodeMenu as a native macOS workspace for snippets, notes, prompts, images, and small developer tools. The central UX challenge was giving different kinds of content a consistent structure while keeping everyday actions quick to reach.

AI helps document snippets, a custom model identifies programming languages, and an MCP server lets coding agents work with the saved knowledge.

Native macOS · Information architecture · Applied AI · MCP integration

Resso · macOS and iOS

Turning connected infrastructure into an interface people can navigate.

Resso translates servers, apps, providers, deployments, and other assets into a visual relationship graph. The work combines data modelling, visualisation, and a consistent experience across Mac and iPhone.

Its MCP server gives AI agents controlled access to the structured data, making that information useful beyond the app itself.

Native macOS and iOS · Data visualisation · Shared Mac and iPhone UX · MCP integration

View the Resso project →

Chitneek · iOS

Bringing reading, scanning, and audio into one iPhone workflow.

Chitneek combines documents, camera scanning, playback, and reading progress in one library. The design challenge was making several kinds of content and interaction feel like one coherent experience.

Local speech models provide high-quality voices entirely offline, without depending on an internet connection.

Native iOS · Document processing · Offline models · Accessible UX

Questions people often have before the first conversation.

If yours is not here, email me or send a message through Upwork.

Is a rough idea enough to get started?

Yes. You do not need wireframes or a formal specification. Tell me who the app is for, what problem it should solve, and what you already know. I can help turn that into a practical first scope.

Do you use AI during development?

Yes. I use AI as a coding tool to build prototypes and explore ideas more quickly, so you can try the product earlier and we can shape it around your vision. I started developing software before these tools existed, and I review all code before it becomes part of the delivered app. I pay particular attention to security and other critical paths, and I remain responsible for the engineering, the quality of the design, and how closely the finished experience matches your expectations.

How do we decide what belongs in the first version?

We focus on the smallest useful experience that can test the idea or improve the current way of working. I will point out which features are essential now, which can wait, and where a simpler choice may save time or cost. The goal is to reach a prototype or MVP you can use as early as possible, then learn from the real experience.

How soon will I be able to test the app?

I aim to provide a working prototype or first build as soon as the core experience is ready. The exact timing depends on the project, but you will not need to wait until the end to try it. You will receive updated builds at agreed points and test them on your own devices.

Can you work on an app that already exists?

Yes. I can add features, improve the interface, investigate bugs, modernise older code, integrate AI, or help prepare an existing app for release. I will first review the relevant parts of the product and codebase, then explain what I recommend and whether fixed-price or $40 hourly work is the better fit.

Who owns the finished code?

You receive the source code and project files created for your product once the agreed work is complete and paid for. Any third-party tools or licensed components will be identified clearly.

What happens after the app is released?

We can agree on a separate plan for fixes, updates, and new features, or I can prepare the project for another developer to continue. The right option depends on how actively you expect the product to change.

Tell me what you would like to make.

A few sentences are enough. Share the idea or current app, who it is for, and what you would like the next useful version to change.
